Grammar Studio · B1

Grammar reference: compare form, meaning, clues and examples from the story or teaching simulations.

1 · Three present tenses at a glance

Aspect / tensePositive / negative / questionWhen do we use it?Example
Present SimpleShe works.
She doesn’t work.
Does she work?		Habits, facts, states and schedules.
usually, every day, often		Angela works in importing and exporting.
Present ContinuousShe is working.
She isn’t working.
Is she working?		Now, temporary situations, changing actions, personal arrangements.
now, at the moment, this week		Kemal is studying in London.
Present Perfect (Simple)She has worked.
She hasn’t worked.
Has she worked?		Life experience, recent results, or an unfinished time period.
ever, never, already, yet, since, for		Have you ever been to Istanbul?
Important contrast: She works in London (usual job) vs She is working in Istanbul now (temporary situation) vs She has worked there for two months (started in the past, relevant now).

2 · Stative verbs

Common stative verbs

know · believe · understand · want · need · prefer · love · hate · own · belong · seem · remember · have (= possess)

Correct: I know Kemal.
Usually incorrect: I am knowing Kemal.

Verbs that change meaning

I think it’s interesting (= opinion).
I’m thinking about my holiday (= mental activity).

She has a shop (= possession).
She’s having lunch (= activity).

I see your point (= understand).
I’m seeing Angela at five (= meeting).

3 · Past Simple

FormStructureExample
Positivesubject + past verb (regular -ed / irregular)Tom picked up his suitcase. / Kemal gave Tom his number.
Negativesubject + didn’t + base verbTom didn’t like airports.
QuestionDid + subject + base verb?Did Tom meet Kemal?
Be in the pastwas / were; wasn’t / weren’t; Was / Were ...?Tom was nervous. / Were they on the plane?

Irregular verbs in the story: sit → sat; take → took; hear → heard; fly → flew; go → went; give → gave; tell → told; leave → left; meet → met.

4 · Be going to

FormStructureExample
Positiveam / is / are + going to + base verbTom is going to take a taxi.
Negativeam / is / are + not + going to + base verbAngela isn’t going to meet him at the airport.
QuestionAm / Is / Are + subject + going to + base verb?Is Tom going to visit Taksim Square?
UseIntention / plan or prediction based on present evidenceLook at those clouds. It is going to rain.
Future contrast: I’m meeting Angela at five = fixed arrangement; I’m going to take a taxi = plan/intention; I’ll carry your bag = decision or offer made now.
